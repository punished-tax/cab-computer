## the plateau

Somewhere around 1200 on chess.com my rating just stopped moving. Not dropping, not climbing — just sitting there like it found a comfortable chair and decided to live in it. For about a month this bothered me. Then I noticed something: I was still having fun every single game, plateau and all.

## what I'm not going to do about it

I'm not going to buy a course. I'm not going to drill 500 tactics puzzles a day. I'm not going to learn the Najdorf twelve moves deep. I looked honestly at what getting to 1600 would cost — hours of study, actual homework — and decided I'd rather spend those hours playing more games badly than fewer games well.

## the one thing that actually helped

Turning off the engine review after losses. Watching a computer tell me my hopeful queen sac was "-6.2, blunder" sucked the joy out of the game faster than anything else I tried. Now I just glance at the result, maybe wince once, and queue up the next game.

## the actual appeal

There's something nice about a game where the entire world is 64 squares and the only thing that matters for the next ten minutes is whether that knight fork is real. It's the closest thing I have to meditation that also lets me occasionally feel like a genius for four moves in a row before immediately blundering my rook.

## verdict

1200 is a fine number. I'll probably still be here next year, still 1200-ish, still enjoying it. Some hobbies are for getting better at. This one's just for playing.
