## the collection

At this point I've got the productivity-app equivalent of a junk drawer. Notion, three separate times, with three separate "this time it's different" workspace setups. A bullet journal that lasted eleven days. A habit tracker with a cute little flame icon that I fed for two weeks before letting it die of neglect, which is a strange thing to feel guilty about.

## the pattern

Every single one of them started the same way: an hour of setup that felt like productivity but was actually just rearranging furniture. New database, new properties, new color-coded tags for priority levels I'd never actually use. By the time the system was "ready," I'd used up the exact burst of motivation that was supposed to go toward the actual tasks.

## what's still alive

A plain text file called `today.txt`. No app, no sync, no tags. I open it, I write down what I'm doing today, I cross things off by literally deleting the line. That's the whole system. It has survived longer than every app I've paid for combined, mostly because there's nothing about it that can go out of style or get a redesign I have to relearn.

## the theory I've landed on

The apps weren't the problem and neither was I — the setup step was. Anything that asks for a plan before it lets you write down a task is asking you to do the fun part (organizing) before the useful part (doing). `today.txt` skips straight to the useful part because there's nothing else it knows how to do.

## verdict

I'm sure I'll download another app eventually. Probably something with a nice icon. But `today.txt` will still be open in a tab next to it, quietly winning.
